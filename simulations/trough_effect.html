Trough Effect

The tongue body makes an unexpected front/back movement during /b/ — an articulatory phenomenon explained by the polar coordinate model

arXiv:2307.02299 §3 Berthommier 2023 T=16, K=10, Kvoy=30, Pexp=1 VLAM (Maeda, 7 params)

What is the trough effect?

In the polar coordinate model, the consonant /b/ is placed at (ρb = 1.0, θb = π/3 — the article position used by the simulations), on the same angular direction as the vowel /u/ (ρu = 1.0, θu = π/3 in synthSYL/phonology.py): with ρb = ρu the two targets coincide exactly; the synthSYL registry default ρb = 1.2 shares the same direction but a larger radius. During the /b/ consonant:

  • the Body parameter (index 1 in the code's 0-based indexing, article parameter 2) is driven by the consonantal trajectory zc(t) toward the /b/ target, which lies on the /u/ direction — its value rises from −2.25 (the /i/ level) toward +1.25 (the Body value of the /u/ direction). Per the Maeda sign convention (negative = fronted/high tongue-body posture of /i/, positive = more retracted), this numerical rise corresponds articulatorily to a posterior and downward displacement of the tongue body relative to the extreme front/high posture of /i/ — a lowering (and backing) with respect to V1, not an elevation;
  • the vowel articulators (Sv = (3, 4, 5, 7) in the article's 1-based numbering; code indices 2, 3, 4, 6 = Dorsum, Tip, LipP, Hy) remain associated with the /i/ target;
  • this interaction produces an unexpected excursion of the Body parameter numerically visible as a rise — articulatorily a tongue-body lowering and backing away from /i/ (the model's counterpart of the Lindblom trough);
  • the selection vector Sc = (1, 2, 6) (article's 1-based numbering; code indices 0, 1, 5 = Jaw, Body, LipH) indicates that the Body parameter, together with Jaw and LipH, is actively driven along the consonantal trajectory for /b/.

This behavior can be related to the trough effect described by Lindblom et al. [29] (reference numbering of arXiv:2307.02299; the manual's bibliography uses \cite keys instead). In their study, the trough effect refers to a momentary deactivation of tongue and/or lip movement following the first vowel and extending into the closure of an intervening labial consonant. The authors interpret this finding as evidence for a segment-by-segment organization of articulatory activity rather than a purely continuous vowel-to-vowel trajectory with an independently superimposed consonantal gesture.

Read with the sign convention in mind, the kinematic pattern produced by the model is more consistent with Lindblom's trough effect than the purely numerical "peak" wording suggests: what the simulation produces during the labial stop is a lowering and backing of the tongue body away from the V1 posture (toward the /u/ direction) — precisely the kinematic signature of the trough, the dorsum leaving the V1 configuration into the closure and returning as V2 approaches.

The model implements no explicit activation/deactivation mechanism: the trough-like pattern emerges from the continuous modulation of the two trajectories zv(t), zc(t) combined with the selection vectors. It can therefore be regarded as a functional analogue of the deactivation described by Lindblom et al., obtained without taking activation/deactivation as a primitive of the control architecture — an interpretive consequence of the present model, not a claim of the original article. It is not a confirmation of the Lindblom mechanism either: the direction is a consequence of the model's placement of /b/ on the /u/ angular direction (a modeling choice); the simulated amplitude (+3.50 in /ibi/) is an order of magnitude larger than the "relatively small" deviations documented only from movement-related data (never acoustically); and the sign of the excursion follows the context (a weak +0.30 bump over /a/, whose Body level already sits near the /u/ direction).

Audio demonstrations (VCV sequences)

Listen to the four VCV sequences. The Body excursion toward /u/ is strong for /ibi/ (+3.50 from the /i/ plateau) and weak for /aba/ (+0.30 from the /a/ plateau, whose Body level +1.00 already sits near the /u/ direction). For /d/ and /g/, the excursion follows their own (different) consonant targets and is much smaller.

/ibi/ — /i/ → /b/ → /i/

Body: −2.25 (/i/) → +1.25 (during /b/, toward the /u/ direction) → −2.25 (/i/)
Excursion toward /u/: 3.50 (most pronounced — the trough effect)

/aba/ — /a/ → /b/ → /a/

Body: +1.00 (/a/) → +1.30 (during /b/) → +1.00 (/a/)
Note: /a/ already lies near the /u/-direction Body value, so the /b/ excursion is small

/idi/ — /i/ → /d/ → /i/

Body: −2.25 (/i/) → −2.26 (during /d/) → −2.25 (/i/)
Note: the /d/ target direction (θd = 23π/16) is not the /u/ direction; Body barely moves because the /d/ Body value almost equals the /i/ one — this shallow dip is not the /u/-directed excursion of /b/

/igi/ — /i/ → /g/ → /i/

Body: −2.25 (/i/) → −1.95 (during the palatal /g/) → −2.25 (/i/)
Note: in /igi/ the palatal /g/ (θ = 23π/12) drives Body slightly toward its own target — a small excursion, not the /u/-directed one of /b/
